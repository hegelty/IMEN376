Term Project Guideline
Production & Operations Management
1  Project Overview
In this term project, each team will select a real-world supply chain, analyze its structure and risks, and identify where AI can improve its operations. The analysis should cover both the overall supply chain (e.g., how materials and information move from raw material suppliers to end customers) and individual stages (e.g., how a specific factory manages its inventory or production schedule). Each team must write the analysis report based on the course material.
The project has three sub-tasks:
Supply Chain Analysis: Identify the key stakeholders (e.g., suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, and retailers) and the flows (materials, information, money) that make up the supply chain. Describe the characteristics and relationships among them.
Risk Analysis: Identify risk factors that can occur during supply chain operations and describe each risk factor in detail. Then, create a concrete and structured risk management plan based on realistic scenarios in which these risks actually occur.
AI Opportunity Discovery: Based on the structural understanding of the supply chain, identify specific points where AI can help solve the problems and risks identified above.
AI opportunities must be based on the supply chain’s actual structure and available data—not in speculation about what AI might generically do. Use course concepts (process analysis, inventory models, forecasting, variability management, scheduling, supply chain coordination, etc.) as the basis for your analysis.

3.1  M1: Supply Chain Selection (Weeks 3–4)
Each team selects a real-world supply chain and develops an initial understanding of its structure. The supply chain should have enough complexity (multiple tiers of suppliers, production facilities, distribution channels, and customer segments) but be narrow enough to analyze within one semester.
Example Tasks
Select and justify a target supply chain for analysis.
Describe the supply chain’s purpose, product/service, stakeholders, and operating environment.
Define the supply chain boundary: what is inside and outside the scope of analysis.
Describe what moves through the supply chain (materials, information, money), why it moves, how it moves (transportation, digital systems, etc.), and how efficiently it moves.
3.2  M2: Supply Chain Structure Modeling & Process Analysis (Weeks 5–6)
Build structured models of the supply chain using course concepts (e.g., process flow diagrams, capacity analysis, inventory models). The goal is to identify the supply chain's structure, bottlenecks, and key decision points with specific numbers (capacity, utilization, flow time, etc.).
Example Tasks
Draw a process flow diagram of the supply chain. Identify the bottleneck and calculate capacity, utilization, and flow time at each stage.
Determine the production strategy (MTS/MTO/ATO) and locate the push–pull boundary. Assess how the company forecasts demand and where forecast errors are largest.
Map inventory across the chain (cycle, safety, pipeline, and seasonal). Evaluate whether current safety stock levels match the target service level, and identify where variability in arrivals, processing times, or quality causes the most disruption.
Examine how information flows across partners. Look for signs of the Bullwhip Effect and evaluate coordination mechanisms in place (contracts, VMI, information sharing).
3.3  M3: Risk Analysis & AI Opportunity Identification (Weeks 9–10)
Systematically identify risks in the supply chain structure, and then discover AI opportunities that help address those risks, based on the analysis from M2.
Part A: Risk Analysis – Example Tasks
Identify risk factors across the supply chain. Categorize them (demand risk, supply risk, process risk, environmental risk, and coordination risk). Link each risk to specific stages, partners, or flows in your supply chain map.
Develop detailed risk scenarios (situations where an identified risk actually occurs). For each scenario, describe: the triggering event, how the problem spreads through the chain, quantitative impact (using your process analysis results), and the effects on upstream and downstream partners.
For each risk scenario, propose resolution strategies using course concepts.
Part B: AI Opportunity Analysis – Example Tasks
Based on your supply chain analysis, identify at least three points where AI can deliver meaningful improvement. Each must be linked to a specific process step, decision point, or data flow in your diagrams, and should address one or more of the risks identified in Part A.
For each candidate, describe what task the AI supports, what improvement is expected (accuracy, speed, cost, risk reduction, etc.), and what data is required.
For each candidate, describe what could go wrong (misprediction, data gaps, model bias, etc.) and what safeguards are needed to keep the system reliable.
3.4  M4: AI Integration Design (Weeks 11–12)
Select the top-ranked AI opportunity from M3 and develop a concrete design for how it would be integrated into the supply chain's operations.
Example Tasks
Justify why you chose this opportunity over the other candidates.
Describe in detail how the AI would work within the current supply chain workflow --- what inputs it receives, what outputs it produces, and who acts on them.
Create a before/after workflow diagram that clearly shows what changes with AI and where the human decision-maker and the AI system interact.
Explain how this AI integration also helps mitigate one or more of the supply chain risks you identified earlier.
3.5  Deliverable Guidelines
Each Deliverable must be submitted in Word, PowerPoint, or PDF format.

The final report integrates and refines all prior work into a single coherent document. It is the primary graded deliverable along with the Proposal Presentation.

Executive Summary
Supply Chain Context & Boundary
Supply Chain Structure & Process Analysis
Risk Analysis: Risk Factors, Risk Scenarios, Resolution Strategies
AI Opportunity Analysis: Discovery, Feasibility Evaluation, and Integration Design

References
The final report is not simply a concatenation of milestone submissions. It should be a revised, integrated document with a consistent style, terminology, and clear logical flow. Earlier milestone work should be improved based on feedback.

5  Project Policies
5.1  Team Composition
Teams of 5 members. Teams will be randomly assigned by the instructor. All team members are expected to contribute equally.
5.2  Generative AI Use Policy
You are permitted and encouraged to use generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot) during this project for brainstorming, drafting, exploring alternatives, and analysis support.
However, the following expectation applies without exception: you must understand everything you submit. If you incorporate AI-generated text, diagrams, or analysis into your deliverables, you are responsible for its correctness, relevance, and coherence. If you cannot explain or defend any part of your submission when asked, it will not receive credit.
5.3  Data Sources
You may use publicly available data, industry reports, news articles, company filings, academic papers, or reasonable estimates based on industry benchmarks. If using estimated data, clearly state your assumptions. Personal experience or interviews with industry practitioners are also acceptable sources.
5.4  Feedback and Support
Feedback on milestone submissions is available by appointment. Do not wait until the final report to ask for guidance.
